## The Camera

Any modern mirrorless or DSLR with **manual exposure control** and **RAW output** will work. What matters most:

- **Sensor size** — Full-frame sensors have a clear noise advantage at high ISO. But modern APS-C and even Micro Four Thirds sensors have improved dramatically.
- **ISO performance** — Check reviews at DPReview for ISO 6400 and 12800 comparisons.
- **Battery life** — Cold nights drain batteries fast. Buy two or three official batteries, not cheap third-party ones.

## The Lens

Wide and fast is the rule:

- **Focal length**: 14mm–24mm (full-frame equivalent)
- **Aperture**: f/2.8 or faster (f/1.8 is ideal)
- **Coma correction**: At wide apertures some lenses produce comet-shaped distortions near the frame edges. Check astrophotography reviews before buying.

Top picks:
- Sigma 14mm f/1.8 Art — the gold standard
- Rokinon/Samyang 14mm f/2.8 — budget champion
- Sony 14mm f/1.8 GM — superb but expensive

## The Tripod

Do not skimp here. A shaky tripod ruins long exposures. You need:

- Carbon fibre or aluminium legs rated to at least 8 kg
- A ball head with an Arca-Swiss compatible clamp
- Spiked feet or rubber feet depending on terrain

## Remote Shutter Release

An intervalometer allows hands-free shutter release, which prevents camera shake at the moment of exposure. It's also essential for:

- Bulb exposures longer than 30 seconds
- Star trail time-lapses (hundreds of frames)
- Bracketed foreground exposures

## Accessories I Never Leave Home Without

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Red headlamp | Preserves night vision |
| Extra batteries (in inner pocket) | Cold kills batteries |
| Lens cloth | Dew and sea spray |
| Hand warmers | Comfort + prevents battery failure |
| Star tracker (optional) | Longer exposures without trailing |

## What You Can Skip

- **Dedicated astronomy cameras** — for wide-field landscape astrophotography, a regular mirrorless is better
- **Goto telescope mounts** — overkill unless you're doing deep-sky imaging
- **UV filters at night** — remove them; they can cause internal reflections from bright stars

## Final Word

The single biggest upgrade to your astrophotography is **location**, not gear. A $500 camera in the Atacama Desert will outperform a $5000 camera in a light-polluted suburb. Save your money for travel.
