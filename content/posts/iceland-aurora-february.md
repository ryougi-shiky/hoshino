## Why February?

Most photographers head to Iceland in September or March — the equinox months offer equal day and night. But February is special. The nights are long, temperatures drop enough to freeze the black-sand beaches, and the tourist crowds thin dramatically.

The trade-off? Cold. Very, very cold. On my second night near Jökulsárlón the wind chill dropped to -22°C. I learned the hard way that battery life halves in those conditions.

## The Itinerary

**Days 1–3: Reykjavík and the Golden Circle**

I landed in Keflavík and drove straight to Þingvellir National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage Site where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates diverge. At night, away from the park's small visitor centre, it is remarkably dark.

**Days 4–6: South Coast**

The south coast is the photographic heart of Iceland:

- Seljalandsfoss waterfall (you can walk behind the curtain of water — magical at blue hour)
- Skógafoss — wide, powerful, often with ice formations in winter
- Reynisfjara black-sand beach with its basalt columns

**Days 7–10: East and the Highlands**

I pushed into the Eastfjords, largely deserted in February. The winding coastal road offers viewpoint after viewpoint of fjords reflecting pale winter light.

## Chasing Aurora

The *KP index* is your number. I had the Aurora Forecast app open constantly. On night five a geomagnetic storm pushed the KP to 6 — the sky erupted.

> "The green ribbons appeared slowly at first, like a dim glow on the horizon. Within 20 minutes they were dancing directly overhead — violet fringes pulsing. I forgot to press the shutter for a full minute."

Settings I used for the aurora:

- **Sony A7S III** at ISO 6400
- **14mm f/1.8** Sigma lens
- 3-second exposures to freeze the movement
- Continuous shooting mode during active bursts

## Gear Notes

Protect your gear from the cold:

- **Hand warmers** inside your bag to keep batteries warm
- **Silica gel packets** — vital when bringing cold gear indoors (condensation can kill electronics)
- **Lens cloth** for the inevitable sea spray on the south coast

## Would I Go Back?

Without hesitation. Iceland in February is demanding but the rewards — otherworldly landscapes, near-solitary access to famous spots, and the very real chance of a major aurora display — are unmatched.
